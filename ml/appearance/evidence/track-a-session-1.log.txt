== the dashed line across painted render a1, traced
  What was seen by eye in the contact sheet: a faint straight horizontal dashed line across the full
  width of every crop. Measured, in the output
  4432d3253e0e1b3706cdaa646b0408a96c71032666565bc92906a139b5cdb8f5:
    a dark band three pixels tall, 615 to 617, deepest at row 616 at 48 grey levels below the tile's
      own median, 610 of its 1024 columns more than 20 levels dark, neutral in all three channels
      (the darkest column is down 172, 166 and 144);
    the strongest period along that line is 128 cycles across the tile, that is exactly every 8.0 px,
      at 21.6 grey levels, and the band is centred exactly on a latent row boundary (616 = 8 x 77);
    8.0 px is the autoencoder's own latent cell pitch, so what the decoder drew is one latent row of
      cells, each rendered as a dark blob;
    the conditioning picture the model was given has no line there: its edge map is dense everywhere
      (450 to 520 pixels a row differing by 40 levels or more) and its own strongest rows are 668,
      426, 337, 40 and 989;
    one output of the 64 carries it.
  That is enough to say what it is NOT: it is not painted content, not a learned overlay, not a
  watermark and not third-party content. Nothing learned from photographs lands on precisely the
  autoencoder's cell pitch and on precisely a latent row boundary. It arose inside this lane's own
  latent pipeline, which makes it a defect of mine and not a provenance question.
  What is NOT settled is which stage. The obvious suspect was my per-step cyclic roll, and for this
  output it fitted: step 12 of 50 shifted the latent rows by 51, and 128 - 51 = 77, the exact latent
  row. The hypothesis then failed its own test over the population: of the strong lines in all 64
  outputs, 47 per cent sit on a boundary that seed's own schedule visited, against 44 per cent
  expected by chance, and 40 per cent are aligned to the 8 px grid against 38 per cent by chance. So
  the single match was luck and the roll is not convicted.
  The latents were not retained, so this cannot be closed from what the run wrote. The next session
  records per-latent-row statistics (min, max, mean) at each step, which costs nothing, and that will
  say whether a row leaves the range the decoder expects and at which step.
  Also learned from the same sweep: the strongest lines in the whole session are paving's joints
  (columns 169 to 171, 511, 854 and rows at the same places), which is the recipe's own 3 by 3 flag
  grid at 1024/6, 1024/2 and 5 x 1024/6. Structure, not a defect, and a reminder to test a line
  against the recipe before calling it an artefact.

== what went wrong, and what it cost
  1. The output directory is a bind mount the host owns, so the container must run as the invoking user.
     First attempt: "PermissionError: [Errno 13] Permission denied: '/out/outputs'". Fixed in
     container/run.sh (--user "$(id -u):$(id -g)").
  2. Triton compiles some of torch's own kernels at run time, so the image needs a C compiler and libc
     headers. First generation: "RuntimeError: Failed to find C compiler" inside torch's
     bmm_outer_product kernel. Fixed in container/Dockerfile (gcc, libc6-dev).
  3. My own tiling wrapper assumed the patched forward returns an object with .sample; this transformer
     answers with a bare tensor. "AttributeError: 'Tensor' object has no attribute 'sample'". Fixed by
     returning the same shape it was given (tuple, .sample, or bare tensor).
     None of the three was findable on the Mac: the dry run exercises the same runner and passes.
  4. About 14 idle minutes, roughly $0.61, spent waiting on a completion watcher instead of reading the
     log when the generation count stopped moving. My own failure, and the second time in one day.
  5. I deleted the instance before pulling the build log and the weights-fetch log, so those two logs no
     longer exist. Every line I quoted from them was quoted while they existed; they cannot be checked
     now. Next time, pull each step's log as that step ends.
  6. The deletion check ran 13 s after the delete request, while the provider still listed the instance
     as DELETING, so scripts/delete-instance.sh printed "IS STILL LISTED" and exited 1, and I recorded
     the deletion at 00:05:56Z without re-listing. The machine was in fact gone within a minute: the
     orchestrator session read the account independently at 2026-09-18T00:06:39Z and got "No instances
     in org glendonchin-hq", which is the earliest and the independent confirmation; my own re-reading
     at 00:20:01Z says the same. The script is what was wrong, and it should wait for the provider's own
     removal rather than a fixed 10 s.
  7. While writing the findings file I typed a digest from memory for painted-render a2 instead of
     copying it. I caught it by rewriting every digest from the picks file and verifying each against
     the bytes on disk: one was wrong, seven were right, and all eight now verify. Nothing was
     published from the wrong value.
  8. The module retention numbers I reported to the orchestrator earlier in the session (brick a1 u
     median 100 per cent, and so on) came from an ad-hoc script with a different normalisation and are
     superseded by the committed measure above. This log's numbers are the ones to use.

== THE FREE TEST, RUN 2026-09-18 ON THIS SESSION'S OWN DATA, AND WHAT IT FOUND
  Session 2 raised a hypothesis: that the edge conditioning role carries the faults, because all four
  faults found there came from it. Rather than propose a machine, the test was run on data already in
  hand: all SIXTEEN brick outputs of this session, looked at at 1:1, four crops each, every texel,
  eight from each role. Records in ml/appearance/look/records-session-1-brick/.
  A SECOND CASE OF LETTERING, in an output nobody had looked at in September: a2 edge seed
  2099328355, output 1e9b5b9e9f13e5c0 by its own record, carries light characters reading "1800 m" on
  a stretcher face. It is refused and cannot be published.
  AND WITH IT THE MECHANISM, which matters more than the role. This session's brick prompt says
  "1800 mm across". Session 2's ashlar prompt says "2400 mm across" and its lettering case painted
  "2400" and "0.00m". THE NUMERALS COME FROM THE PROMPT. Both prompts also said, in words, no text,
  and were obeyed on that and not on this. A model paints the numbers it is told.
  The rule now: a job spec carries NO numerals in a prompt at all. The conditioning picture already
  gives the model the structure and its scale, and the recipe holds the true dimensions for anyone
  who needs them. tests/test_runner.py holds it, and names track-a-session-1, track-a-session-2 and
  track-a-smoke as grandfathered WITH their risk, because correcting them would make this evidence
  cite specs that never ran; the test also fails if someone quietly corrects one.
  THE ROLE TALLY, over the 32 module-material outputs now looked at at 1:1 (brick here, ashlar in
  session 2):
    edge, 16 outputs: 2 definite lettering cases, 1 ambiguous, 2 inventions of block geometry
    depth, 16 outputs: no lettering, no invented geometry; the a2 depth outputs carry an
      over-saturated green along the joints, which is a fault of another kind
  So the edge role carries every case of learned text and every invented geometry across two targets
  and both models. That is a consistent pattern on 32 outputs, not a proof, and the mechanism above
  explains the CONTENT of the text without explaining why the role matters. A signal strong enough to
  reproduce a structure may be strong enough to invite elaboration on it; that part is still a
  hypothesis.
  WHY THIS STOPPED AT BRICK, as a decision rather than an omission: the sixteen brick outputs are a
  complete balanced test of the role hypothesis on one target, eight per role, and they produced the
  mechanism. Once the mechanism was in hand, acting on it beat counting further: the guard against
  numerals in prompts protects every future session, while another sixteen crops of paving would only
  have refined a rate. Paving costs nothing to do later and the sheets are already cut for it.
  WHAT IS STILL UNLOOKED AT: this session's other 48 outputs (footway paving 16, painted render 16,
  carriageway asphalt 16) and session 2's other 24. Nothing from them may be published. THE
  NO-NUMERALS RULE DOES NOT RETROACTIVELY CLEAN THEM: those prompts carried numerals too, so the
  risk that produced both known cases was present in every generation of both sessions. The eight
  picks looked at in September are the only other outputs of this session anyone has seen at full
  resolution, and the sentence "no lettering in 32 crops" covers those crops and nothing wider.
