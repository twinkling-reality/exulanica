WHAT A CARRIED FRAME SAVES FOR A CATALOG PERSON, 2026-09-17.

What ran: the development preview in headless Chrome 152 on an Apple M3 Pro, canvas 1440x900, with 24
catalog people loaded and drawn in full (none pending, none far). Six blocks; each block calls
pose({position, deltaSeconds}) once per person for 400 iterations and follow(position) the same
number of times, and the two arms swap order every block so a warming cache or a drifting machine
cannot favour one. Figures are microseconds of the renderable's own work per person per call.

Both machine-wide slots were held, but the one-minute load was 17.9, above the 8 under which absolute
timings here are trustworthy. That does not touch the comparison: the two arms run milliseconds apart
inside one block and alternate across blocks, and the six blocks agree to within 0.26 microseconds for
pose. Read the absolute microseconds as upper bounds.

RESULT: a solved pose costs about 2.6 microseconds per person (2.55 to 2.81 across blocks). A carried
frame costs about 0.1 microseconds (0.05 to 0.15), which is the page clock's own resolution floor over
9,600 calls, so it is an upper bound rather than a measurement. Carrying therefore saves about
2.5 microseconds per person per carried frame: 0.06 ms a frame with 24 people posed every frame
against 0.003 ms with all of them carried.

WHAT THAT MEANS, AND IT IS NOT WHAT I EXPECTED: a catalog person costs 0.18 ms of frame work at the
95th percentile (see frame-budget-2026-09-17.log.txt), so the pose solve is about 1.5 per cent of what
that person costs. The rest is the engine's own work: the animation graph, the bone matrices and the
skinning, which run for every drawn person whether or not anyone posed them. Posing on fewer frames is
worth having and costs nothing to offer, but it is not a lever on the character budget. The levers
would be fewer mesh instances or bones per person, or stopping a distant person's animation, and each
of those changes what the person looks like, so each needs its own decision and its own measurement.
