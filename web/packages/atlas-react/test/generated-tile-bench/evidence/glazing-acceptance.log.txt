GLAZING ACCEPTANCE, texture proposal section 3, 2026-09-17, lane/tile-runtime-glazing (stacked on the cutout head 6d650699)
Driver: headless Chrome 152 at 1440x900 against the bench (tile-runtime-bench, 5191), ANGLE Metal on Apple M3 Pro,
look exulanica.generated-tile-look@1 with the camera frame's scene colour copy on (requested by the glazing binding).
Frames pumped synchronously through window.bench; pixels read back from the default framebuffer; luminance Rec. 709.

INPUTS, BOTH TEST-ONLY (test/generated-tile-bench/test-glazing.ts), never shipped:
- Glass: clean float glass shaped as a v2 procedural glazing set, 512 x 512 texels over 2000 mm, tint (246, 250, 247),
  transmission 255, roughness 5 (20 in 1000), ior_millionths 1500000, bound through TileTextureUploads.adopt.
  The published cc0.float-glazing is NOT measured: texture batch 1 is not on main yet.
- Backing: a TEST-ONLY surface standing in for what the grammar will state lies behind glazing (a vitrine or an interior
  backing, not yet in the grammar): a lit black and white checker of 200 mm squares, 20 m wide, 0.6 m behind the pane.
  Level 1 is the checker in open light; level 0.25 scales its diffuse to a quarter, nearer a shop interior's brightness.

SCENE (bench.measureGlazing): a 2 m pane facing -Z at (0, 40, 0), drawn with the runtime's glazing material and casting
shadows as the runtime decides for its class (none). Per view: the pane's footprint (pane drawn as an opaque mask), and
frames of the backing alone (C), the backing seen through the pane (A), and the pane over a matte black backing (R, the
reflection alone).

1. THE FITOUT READS THROUGH CLEAN GLASS, 2 TO 6 m, face on. Correlation of luminance of A with C inside the footprint, and
   the share of C's contrast (standard deviation) A keeps.
   backing level 1:     2 m  corr 0.998  contrast 0.957    4 m  0.998  0.935    6 m  0.996  0.915
   backing level 0.25:  2 m  corr 0.967  contrast 0.957    4 m  0.969  0.905    6 m  0.980  0.863
   Met: the checker keeps its pattern and at least 86 per cent of its contrast through the glass at every distance.

2. THE SKY'S REFLECTION DOMINATES PAST 70 DEGREES. At 4 m, the camera swung about the pane's vertical axis; reflection share
   is mean luminance of R over mean luminance of A in the footprint.
   angle from normal        0     30     50     60     70     75     80     85
   backing level 1        0.164  0.168  0.184  0.234  0.376  0.488  0.621  0.738
   backing level 0.25     0.319  0.355  0.412  0.508  0.701  0.810  0.901  0.958
   With the interior-bright backing the reflection is half the light at 60 degrees and 70 per cent at 70: met. Against the
   sunlit checker, as bright as the street outside, reflection passes half only beyond 75 degrees.
   Reflection alone (mean luminance of R) with the runtime's reflectance: 25.5 at 0 degrees, 60.3 at 70, 139.2 at 85.
   Before the glazing material replaced PlayCanvas's Schlick term (whose value at grazing incidence is capped at
   max(gloss^2 * F0, F0), 0.04 for glass), the same reflection-only frames read 25.5 at 0 degrees and 24.4 at 85: flat.

3. GLAZING CASTS NO SHADOW (bench.measureGlazingShadow). A horizontal 4 m pane 2.5 m above lit paving, sun straight down,
   1,600 ground samples: open mean 226.8, under the pane 226.8, darkened by 0.000.
   An earlier run whose bench helper cast shadows for every class darkened the ground by 0.593 and put a shadow of the
   pane on the backing; both were bench errors, and every figure above is from after the fix.
